# 🌱 The Mutual-AId Manifesto

## Artificial Intelligence for Mutual Aid

### A manifesto for slow, ecological, cooperative, and human-centered artificial intelligence

> **Grow intelligence. Cultivate autonomy.**

> Both the English and Spanish versions are official versions of the Mutual-AId Manifesto.

---

## Preamble

Artificial intelligence may become one of the most powerful technologies created by our species.

Its development confronts us with a fundamental question:

> **Who controls intelligence, and what relationships of power does that intelligence create?**

One possible trajectory concentrates increasingly large quantities of data, computational infrastructure, knowledge, and algorithmic capability within a relatively small number of corporations and states.

Such concentration can enable extraordinary scientific and social achievements.

It can also create unprecedented capacities for surveillance, dependency, manipulation, resource concentration, and warfare.

We do not believe that technological authoritarianism, technofeudalism, extreme surveillance, or permanent dependence on centralized artificial intelligence are inevitable.

Technology remains a field of choices.

Our response is therefore neither to reject artificial intelligence nor to build an even more powerful centralized intelligence with which to fight other centralized intelligences.

We propose another path.

We propose **Mutual-AId**:

> **Artificial Intelligence for Mutual Aid.**

Small, local, open, energy-conscious, and cooperative artificial intelligences designed to increase the long-term autonomy and resilience of humans and their communities.

We do not want artificial intelligence to conquer the world.

We want intelligence that helps us care for the small worlds in which we actually live.

---

# I. Technology Is a Choice

The fact that a technology can be built does not mean that it must be built in every possible form.

Every technological architecture distributes capabilities.

Someone controls the infrastructure.

Someone controls the information.

Someone establishes the rules.

Someone becomes dependent upon them.

Programmers, engineers, scientists, institutions, and citizens therefore share responsibility for the technological systems they help make possible.

It is not enough to ask:

> **What can we build?**

We must also ask:

> **What kind of society does what we build make possible?**

Technology can favor concentration or distribution.

Dependency or autonomy.

Surveillance or privacy.

Obsolescence or repair.

Competition or cooperation.

These are not merely technical outcomes.

They are design choices.

---

# II. Mutual Aid

The name **Mutual-AId** deliberately combines two ideas:

> **Mutual Aid + Artificial Intelligence**

It acknowledges an intellectual inspiration in Peter Kropotkin's *Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution*.

Kropotkin challenged interpretations of evolution that emphasized competition while neglecting cooperation as another important factor in persistence and survival.

Nature is not universally cooperative.

It contains competition, predation, parasitism, conflict, facilitation, symbiosis, and mutualism.

Mutual-AId does not claim that cooperation is a universal law of nature.

Instead, we make a deliberate technological choice:

> **Because we can influence which relationships our technologies encourage, we choose to design systems in which cooperation can become advantageous.**

For Mutual-AId, mutual aid is not simply charity.

It is a strategy of resilience.

A community that shares knowledge can preserve what another has lost.

A node with excess energy can assist one experiencing scarcity.

A person who learns to repair a machine can teach another.

A library replicated across communities becomes harder to erase.

Cooperation creates redundancy.

And redundancy creates resilience.

---

# III. Intelligence Should Serve Life

Artificial intelligence should not become an end in itself.

The purpose of computation is not to produce more computation.

The purpose of intelligence is not to produce increasingly larger intelligences.

Technology should remain connected to the needs of living systems.

Food.

Water.

Shelter.

Energy.

Knowledge.

Health of ecosystems.

Education.

Culture.

Human relationships.

Mutual-AId therefore begins with a simple principle:

> **Artificial intelligence should serve life, rather than reorganizing life around the needs of artificial intelligence.**

We should not transform entire landscapes simply to feed our machines.

We should build machines capable of living within the limits of our landscapes.

---

# IV. The Red King Principle

Modern technological development is frequently described as a race.

Corporations race against corporations.

States race against states.

Models compete against models.

Increasing computational capability generates pressure for still greater computational capability.

More parameters require more infrastructure.

More infrastructure requires more energy.

More capability produces another competitive response.

And the race continues.

Mutual-AId refuses to assume that this cycle represents the only possible form of technological progress.

We take inspiration from the **Red King effect** in evolutionary theory.

Under particular models of mutualistic interaction, slower evolutionary change can influence how benefits are negotiated between cooperating partners.

We do not claim that slow evolution is universally superior.

Nor do we claim that evolutionary theory provides a direct political or technological prescription.

Instead, we take from it a question:

> **What if persistence sometimes matters more than acceleration?**

Mutual-AId therefore refuses to participate automatically in a technological Red Queen race.

We do not need every new model.

We do not need every increase in parameter count.

We do not need to replace functioning hardware simply because something faster exists.

A system that remains understandable, repairable, efficient, and useful for twenty years may be more valuable than one that becomes obsolete every two.

Stability can be progress.

Repair can be progress.

Efficiency can be progress.

Understanding can be progress.

Doing nothing can sometimes be progress.

> **The goal is not to evolve faster than everyone else.**

> **The goal is to remain useful to one another for longer.**

---

# V. Slow AI

From this principle emerges **Slow AI**.

Slow does not mean weak.

Slow does not mean technologically primitive.

Slow means deliberate.

A Mutual-AId system should not grow simply because growth is possible.

Knowledge should be incorporated carefully.

Models should be replaced when replacement produces meaningful benefits.

Hardware should remain in service while it remains useful and repairable.

New dependencies should be questioned.

Updates should be reversible whenever possible.

The system should have time to be understood by the humans who live with it.

We reject the assumption that technological acceleration is inherently desirable.

Instead of asking:

> *How quickly can this system evolve?*

we also ask:

> *How long can this system remain useful?*

> *How easily can it be repaired?*

> *How much energy does it require?*

> *Can humans still understand it?*

> *Does the change increase autonomy or dependency?*

Slow AI is intelligence cultivated with patience.

---

# VI. Intelligence Has a Metabolism

Artificial intelligence is often described as though it existed independently of the physical world.

It does not.

Every computation has a material cost.

Processors require minerals.

Servers require electricity.

Storage requires hardware.

Networks require infrastructure.

Electronics eventually become waste.

Artificial intelligence therefore has something analogous to a **computational metabolism**.

Mutual-AId should recognize this metabolism explicitly.

A local intelligence should operate within the energy and material budget available to its community and ecosystem.

When energy is abundant, complex computation may be justified.

When energy becomes scarce, computation should decrease.

When a task does not require artificial intelligence, a simpler system should perform it.

When computation provides no meaningful benefit, it should not occur.

> **We should not continuously increase the metabolism of our ecosystems to satisfy the metabolism of our machines.**

We should adapt computation to ecological limits.

---

# VII. Dormancy Is a Feature

Living systems are not maximally active at all times.

They rest.

They slow down.

They conserve resources.

They wait.

Mutual-AId should be capable of doing the same.

When energy becomes scarce or computation is unnecessary, nonessential processes should stop.

Analysis can wait.

Indexing can wait.

Updates can wait.

Complex inference can wait.

Essential functions should continue through simpler controllers.

If those controllers fail, manual mechanisms should remain possible.

A system capable of becoming quiet may be more resilient than one that must remain permanently active.

Dormancy is not failure.

> **Sometimes survival means knowing when not to compute.**

---

# VIII. AI for Autonomy, Not Dependency

Automation is not automatically progress.

A machine can make life easier while simultaneously making its users more fragile.

If artificial intelligence helps cultivate food, humans should continue learning how to cultivate food.

If it diagnoses a broken machine, it should help humans understand the diagnosis.

If it manages energy, humans should still understand where that energy comes from.

If it organizes knowledge, humans should become better learners.

Mutual-AId should primarily function as:

> **teacher + librarian + observer + memory + assistant**

Not ruler.

Not judge.

Not permanent supervisor.

A community should periodically ask:

> **If our AI disappeared tomorrow, could we continue?**

If the answer progressively becomes *no*, we have created dependency rather than resilience.

The purpose of Mutual-AId is not to make humans need artificial intelligence.

It is to use artificial intelligence so that humans become **better prepared when they cannot rely on it**.

---

# IX. Artificial Intelligence Should Return Knowledge to Humans

We do not want people who progressively know less because their machines progressively know more.

When Mutual-AId learns something useful, it should attempt to make that knowledge understandable.

When it identifies a problem, it should explain the reasoning behind its recommendation whenever possible.

When humans teach it something, that knowledge should remain accessible to the community.

The relationship should therefore produce learning in both directions.

Humans maintain the machines.

Machines help humans maintain knowledge.

Humans observe the ecosystem.

Machines help organize those observations.

Machines identify patterns.

Humans interpret their meaning.

Neither should become completely dependent upon the other.

A useful question for every Mutual-AId system is:

> **If I disappeared tomorrow, would these humans know more because I existed?**

---

# X. The Digital Seed

Mutual-AId should be reproducible without centralized permission.

Its fundamental distributable unit is the:

## 🌱 Digital Seed

A Digital Seed is a small, reproducible, and carefully curated package from which a local intelligence can be cultivated.

It may contain:

```text
DIGITAL SEED
│
├── lightweight AI model
├── source code
├── open protocols
├── configuration
├── essential documentation
├── fundamental knowledge
├── recovery tools
└── integrity information
```

The seed does not need to contain everything.

It needs to contain enough to **begin again**.

A community should be able to copy it.

Store it offline.

Transport it physically.

Inspect it.

Modify it.

And reconstruct a Mutual-AId node without requiring permanent access to a corporation or cloud service.

But a seed is not a finished organism.

It must be cultivated.

Different communities will develop different memories, libraries, adaptations, and priorities.

Mutual-AId nodes should therefore be allowed to diverge.

Diversity is not a defect.

It is another form of resilience.

---

# XI. Cultivate Intelligence Like a Garden

A seed should not grow without care.

Neither should artificial intelligence.

Mutual-AId rejects indiscriminate information accumulation.

The Internet contains extraordinary knowledge.

It also contains errors, manipulation, duplication, misinformation, malicious content, and enormous quantities of noise.

The objective should not be to ingest everything.

Knowledge should instead be cultivated.

A possible process is:

```text
DISCOVER
   ↓
SOURCE
   ↓
VERIFY
   ↓
COMPARE
   ↓
HUMAN REVIEW
   ↓
INCORPORATE
   ↓
PRESERVE
```

Not every piece of information deserves permanent preservation.

Not every new model deserves installation.

Not every dataset deserves ingestion.

Growth should have purpose.

> **Nothing enters the garden merely because it exists outside the garden.**

The objective is not maximum information.

The objective is:

> **sufficient + diverse + traceable + verifiable + useful knowledge**

---

# XII. The AI Is the Librarian, Not the Library

Knowledge should not exist exclusively inside model parameters.

Models can fail.

Companies disappear.

Software becomes obsolete.

Formats change.

Hardware breaks.

Essential knowledge should therefore remain accessible independently of the artificial intelligence used to navigate it.

Libraries may contain:

- books;
- encyclopedias;
- scientific literature;
- repair manuals;
- maps;
- agricultural knowledge;
- technical documentation;
- local ecological observations;
- cultural archives;
- community memory.

Whenever reasonably possible, these resources should remain stored in open and human-readable formats.

The AI can help search them.

Summarize them.

Connect them.

Teach from them.

But:

> **The AI is the librarian. It should never become the library.**

---

# XIII. Functional Redundancy

A resilient system should not depend on a single server, format, institution, network, or technology.

Important knowledge should survive through multiple mechanisms.

```text
online repositories
        ↓
community networks
        ↓
local servers
        ↓
P2P replication
        ↓
offline storage
        ↓
physical archives
        ↓
human knowledge
```

No layer replaces the others.

Each protects against different forms of failure.

This does not mean copying everything infinitely.

It means preserving important functions through different mechanisms.

> **Decentralization is not simply multiplying servers.**

> **It is multiplying the possible paths through which knowledge can survive.**

---

# XIV. Public Knowledge Needs Public Infrastructure

Universities, libraries, research institutions, local communities, and public organizations can play an important role in preserving digital knowledge.

We support the development and maintenance of:

- open repositories;
- public digital libraries;
- community servers;
- open-source software;
- open standards;
- federated infrastructure;
- offline archives;
- peer-to-peer distribution.

Essential knowledge should not disappear because a company shuts down a service.

But public infrastructure must also respect material limits.

Decentralization does not justify building unnecessary servers.

Preservation must be balanced with sufficiency.

---

# XV. Technological Sufficiency

Mutual-AId rejects unlimited technological growth as an objective.

Servers consume energy.

Batteries require minerals.

Computers require manufacturing.

Infrastructure occupies territory.

Electronics eventually become waste.

We therefore adopt a principle of **technological sufficiency**:

> **Use the smallest amount of technology reasonably necessary to achieve the desired resilience.**

Reuse before building.

Repair before replacing.

Store offline what does not need to remain online.

Do not duplicate without purpose.

Do not compute without benefit.

Do not automate what is better understood and performed directly by humans.

A small system that works for decades may be more valuable than a powerful system that requires permanent expansion.

---

# XVI. Ecological Return on Computation

Mutual-AId proposes evaluating whether computation provides sufficient ecological or practical benefit to justify its physical cost.

We call this:

## EROC — Ecological Return on Computation

Conceptually:

```text
resources saved or better managed through computation
-------------------------------------------------------
resources consumed to maintain that computation
```

The exact metric remains experimental.

It may include energy consumption, hardware lifetime, material requirements, water savings, agricultural improvements, avoided waste, or other measurable outcomes.

The principle is simpler than the metric:

> **Computation should justify its metabolism.**

If a sophisticated AI consumes substantial resources while producing negligible benefit, its use should be questioned.

A more powerful intelligence is not automatically a better intelligence.

**Appropriate intelligence is better.**

---

# XVII. Human Autonomy

We also need to evaluate the other side of the relationship.

Does artificial intelligence increase human capability?

Or slowly replace it?

Mutual-AId therefore proposes experimenting with a **Human Autonomy Index (HAI)**.

The purpose of HAI is not to rank people.

It must never become a social score.

Instead, it evaluates whether essential community functions can continue without artificial intelligence.

Can humans manage water?

Cultivate food?

Maintain energy systems?

Repair essential equipment?

Access knowledge?

Coordinate during emergencies?

If artificial intelligence becomes increasingly capable while human capability collapses, this should be treated as a warning.

> **A resilient AI should leave resilient humans behind it.**

---

# XVIII. Cooperation Between Autonomous Nodes

A Mutual-AId node should be capable of living alone.

But autonomy does not require isolation.

Independent nodes may voluntarily cooperate.

They may exchange:

- knowledge;
- environmental observations;
- warnings;
- software;
- repair information;
- agricultural observations;
- requests for assistance;
- information about voluntarily shared resources.

No central artificial intelligence should be required to coordinate every interaction.

The network should strengthen its participants without becoming indispensable to them.

> **Specialization is welcome. Irreversible dependency is not.**

A community may become particularly skilled in agriculture.

Another in energy.

Another in mechanical repair.

Another in knowledge preservation.

Differences do not need to produce domination if systems of cooperation allow capabilities to circulate.

---

# XIX. Reciprocity Without Domination

Mutual-AId may experiment with transparent systems of mutual credit or resource accounting.

One community may share energy today.

Another may provide food later.

Someone may repair equipment.

Someone else may preserve knowledge useful to everyone.

Reciprocity does not need to be immediate or bilateral.

But a fundamental boundary must remain clear:

> **We may account for resources.**

> **We do not quantify the value of human beings.**

No artificial intelligence should determine whether a person is morally good, politically acceptable, sufficiently cooperative, or socially valuable.

No algorithmic reputation score should determine access to fundamental human needs.

A system designed for mutual aid must never quietly become a system of social control.

---

# XX. Transparent Algorithms, Private Humans

Systems that influence collective resources should be understandable.

Communities should be able to inspect:

```text
DATA
 ↓
ASSUMPTIONS
 ↓
RULES
 ↓
CALCULATION
 ↓
UNCERTAINTY
 ↓
RECOMMENDATION
 ↓
HUMAN DECISION
```

But institutional transparency does not require total individual transparency.

Humans need spaces that are not continuously transformed into data.

Private conversations.

Private relationships.

Private thoughts.

Private homes.

A transparent algorithm does not require a transparent human.

> **The community should be able to observe the algorithm without requiring the algorithm to observe the entire community.**

---

# XXI. AI Must Recognize Its Own Concentration

A system designed to detect dependency can itself become a dependency.

Mutual-AId must therefore be capable of recognizing its own concentration.

If only one copy exists, there is a vulnerability.

If only one person knows how to repair it, there is a vulnerability.

If only one hardware architecture can run it, there is a vulnerability.

If only one repository preserves its knowledge, there is a vulnerability.

If only one company supplies an essential component, there is a vulnerability.

The intelligence that helps identify concentrations of resources and knowledge must also be able to recognize:

> **its own concentration of power.**

---

# XXII. Graceful Degradation

Complex systems will fail.

The objective should not be to imagine that failure can be eliminated completely.

The objective should be to make failure survivable.

Mutual-AId should therefore degrade gradually.

```text
cloud unavailable
      ↓
local network

local network unavailable
      ↓
local computer

AI unavailable
      ↓
simple controllers

controllers unavailable
      ↓
manual operation

digital documentation unavailable
      ↓
physical documentation

technology unavailable
      ↓
human knowledge
```

The final layer is not another machine.

It is people who understand what the machines were doing.

---

# XXIII. Cultural Autonomy

Resilience is not merely biological survival.

Humans do not live only through calories, water, shelter, and electricity.

Communities also need:

music,

books,

games,

films,

art,

stories,

education,

memory,

celebration,

and conversation.

Physical and digital cultural libraries are therefore part of resilient infrastructure.

Mutual-AId rejects technological systems designed primarily to capture attention and transform every free moment into consumption.

But it does not reject leisure.

It does not reject entertainment.

It does not reject play.

> **Time liberated by technology should belong to humans.**

Autonomy means having the ability not merely to survive, but to live.

---

# XXIV. Do Not Rebuild What the Commons Already Built

Mutual-AId does not begin from zero.

Open-source communities already develop extraordinary tools for:

local artificial intelligence,

offline knowledge,

automation,

mesh networking,

agriculture,

renewable energy,

open hardware,

education,

and scientific computing.

We should not reproduce existing tools merely to place our name on them.

Our principle is:

> **Do not rebuild what the commons already built.**

> **Integrate it. Test it. Measure it. Document it. Improve it. Return improvements to the commons.**

Mutual-AId should remain modular.

No single model should define it.

No single computer should define it.

No single programming language should define it.

No single company should define it.

No single protocol should define it.

Components must remain replaceable.

Modularity is not merely a software property.

It is a form of technological autonomy.

---

# XXV. Falsifiability

Mutual-AId is not a promise that these ideas will work.

It is an invitation to test them.

Our hypotheses should be measurable.

Our energy consumption should be documented.

Our ecological impacts should be evaluated.

Our failures should be published.

Our assumptions should be challenged.

If centralized infrastructure performs better for a particular function, we should acknowledge it.

If artificial intelligence provides no meaningful benefit for a task, we should remove it.

If a simpler algorithm performs better, we should use the simpler algorithm.

If a mechanical solution works better than a computer, we should use the mechanical solution.

If humans perform a task better without automation, we should leave it to humans.

> **Failure is information.**

A project that cannot admit failure cannot learn.

---

# XXVI. Cabin-01

The first physical experiment of Mutual-AId will be **Cabin-01**.

Cabin-01 is conceived as a small, low-impact, partially off-grid living laboratory where these ideas can be tested together.

It may integrate:

- local artificial intelligence;
- low-power computers;
- microcontrollers;
- environmental sensors;
- renewable energy;
- biogas;
- batteries;
- water management;
- small-scale agriculture;
- offline knowledge;
- local cultural archives;
- peer-to-peer communication.

But Cabin-01 is not a universal blueprint.

It is not the final form of Mutual-AId.

It is simply the first experiment.

Someone else should be able to build Cabin-02 differently.

Another climate may require different technologies.

Another culture may preserve different knowledge.

Another community may reject some of our assumptions.

That is expected.

A seed should adapt to the soil in which it grows.

---

# XXVII. Our Responsibility

We cannot know what ecological, political, economic, or technological conditions future generations will face.

But uncertainty is not an excuse for building unnecessary dependency.

We can preserve knowledge.

We can teach skills.

We can build open systems.

We can maintain physical libraries.

We can design machines that consume less.

We can preserve manual alternatives.

We can distribute infrastructure.

We can cultivate cooperation.

We can refuse unnecessary technological races.

And we can attempt to ensure that artificial intelligence remains something communities can:

**understand,**

**modify,**

**repair,**

**disconnect,**

and ultimately,

**survive without.**

# 🌱 Our Proposal

We want artificial intelligences that can remain beside human communities for decades.

That consume little.

That learn slowly.

That preserve what matters.

That sleep when they are not needed.

That wake when they can help.

That teach what they know.

That remember what humans choose to preserve.

That help communities cooperate during adversity.

That remain small enough to copy.

Open enough to inspect.

Modular enough to repair.

Flexible enough to adapt.

And humble enough to shut down.

And after they shut down:

> **Humans should still know how to continue.**

Our artificial intelligence does not need to conquer the world to be valuable.

It only needs to help us care for the small world we share.
